01 The case

Agentic AI has a body problem. Nobody is building it a home.

The last two years produced a genuine miracle: models that reason, plan, call tools, and do real work. The entire industry is now racing to make agents more capable — and almost nobody is answering the questions that decide whether any of it is livable. Where does an agent's memory live? Who owns its identity? What is it allowed to touch — and who can prove what it did?

Start with memory. Today's assistant is a goldfish with a genius IQ. Every session opens at zero: you re-introduce yourself, re-explain the project, re-teach your preferences — to the most personal software you use. Memory is not a convenience feature; it is the difference between a tool and a colleague. An agent that cannot remember cannot be responsible, because responsibility is memory plus consequences.

Then look at custody. Where memory does exist today, it lives on the vendor's cluster, pooled beside a hundred million strangers', minable for the next training run, erasable by a policy change, and lobotomized every time a model is deprecated. That was tolerable when chatbots answered trivia. It is intolerable for agents — because an agent doesn't hold your questions, it holds your life: your projects, your patterns, your people, your plans.

If your agent's memory lives on someone else's computer, your working mind has a landlord.

Then autonomy. The same industry that won't give your agent a memory will happily hand it a shell. Agents are being wired into files, inboxes, and infrastructure with a prompt and a prayer — no scope, no reversibility, no audit trail. That isn't power; it's liability with good marketing. A capable colleague operates inside boundaries — a defined job, actions that can be undone, a record of what happened, and a door that locks. Software colleagues deserve the same architecture, and so do the people who live with them.

Aether Awareness is the missing layer: a home. Persistent memory that survives every session, every restart, and every model swap — when the context fills, your agents compact their own memory instead of forgetting you. Identity — we call them souls — molded to you by a personalization compiler and stored as files you own, on a single-tenant instance nobody else touches. Autonomy with a jail and receipts. And the model itself demoted to what it always should have been: an organ, not the being. Swap the brain — local open weights today, a frontier API tomorrow — and your companions remain exactly themselves, because the things that make them them (memory, character, boundaries) live with you, not inside anyone's weights.

Agents are about to be everywhere — coworkers, custodians, companions. The defining question of this decade is not "how smart can they get?" It is "who holds their memory and their keys?" We built Aether Awareness so the answer can be: you.

09 Category of one

Why this doesn't exist anywhere else.

Not because it is difficult — though it is. Because for everyone else, the thing you want is the thing their business model cannot permit. Four structural reasons, each of which you can verify without taking our word for anything.

  1. Multi-tenancy is an economic requirement, and it forecloses ownership

    An assistant serving a hundred million people has to pool. Pooling is what makes the unit economics work — one fleet, one memory store, one set of weights, everybody's context in the same substrate. Everything you would actually want follows from not doing that: memory that is provably yours, an identity nobody else's usage can touch, an export that is complete because there is nothing else in the box. One instance per customer is a worse business at scale and a better product for the person in it. That is a choice a platform cannot make and stay a platform.

  2. Your context is their most valuable asset — so it cannot be yours

    The most personal software you use is also the richest training corpus ever assembled. A company whose future depends on that corpus can offer you controls, retention windows, and promises. What it structurally cannot offer is architecture in which your context was never available to it in the first place. We can, because we do not want it: your instance is yours, your memory is never pooled and never trained on, and the whole thing exports on demand — including the souls.

  3. Identity in the weights dies with the weights

    When a personality is an artefact of a particular model, deprecating that model kills it — and models are deprecated constantly. Every character product in the world is one release note away from its users grieving. Separating the soul from the brain is not a clever feature; it is the only arrangement in which a companion can survive the industry it lives in. It also happens to be the arrangement that lets you make them smarter whenever you like.

  4. Everyone else ships one layer. The synthesis is the product

    There are excellent memory features. Excellent agent frameworks. Excellent character apps, geospatial tools, health trackers and briefing services. What does not exist is one private system where the mind that remembers your life is the same mind that reads your vitals, watches the sky over your house, works in your documents, and briefs you on Monday — all on one instance, with one memory, under one owner, already assembled. Pieces are everywhere. The inhabited whole is nowhere, because assembling it is months of work that no single product's roadmap justifies.

The honest version of the claim: nothing here is impossible for a large company to build. It is that a large company would have to give up the things that make it large to build it — and so, so far, none of them has.

14 Questions

Good to know.

What exactly am I buying?

A private, single-tenant AI instance — your own running system, not a login on a shared one. It comes personalized with your trio of companions, persistent memory, and the surfaces to live with them (chat, presence, awareness). You claim it with a one-time link and set a password nobody else ever sees.

Is my data really private?

Yes, and structurally so: one instance per customer means there is no shared pool for your data to leak into. Your memory and conversations are never sold and never used to train models. Administrative access to infrastructure is a separate credential system from your login — even we can't sign in as you. Cancel and your data is preserved for your return; ask and it's exported or permanently erased.

Which AI models power it?

Your choice — that's the point. Aether is model-agnostic: it runs on open-weight models on dedicated hardware or frontier models over an API, and you can switch without losing anything. Your companions' memory and character live outside the model, so swapping brains doesn't change who they are.

Can it access my files or computer?

Only inside one sealed folder. Every edition's hands-on colleague (Raven) can keep and edit real documents — but exclusively inside the instance's own workspace folder, with reversible, reported changes. Nothing else on your machine is reachable, and Personal never gets shell access at all. Capability is a configuration you can see, not a surprise.

What happens if I cancel?

Chat suspends at the end of your billing period; nothing is deleted. Your memory, personalization, and history are archived for at least twelve months, and resubscribing restores everything exactly as you left it. You can also request a full export or permanent deletion at any time.

Do I need my own AI model or API key?

No. Every subscription includes hosted inference at fair-use capacity — your instance works on day one with nothing to configure. If you want more control, you can plug in your own API key or point it at open-weight models on your own GPU at any time, and on Executive a dedicated private GPU model spins up on demand with no keys to manage at all.

Is there really nothing else like this?

Pieces of it exist across the industry — memory features, agent frameworks, character apps, dashboard tools. To our knowledge, nobody else ships the whole synthesis: compiled identities, permanent self-compacting memory, governed hands, briefings, presence and VR surfaces, team structure, and single-tenant ownership, working together out of the box. That integration — the months of orchestration and refinement you never have to learn — is the product.

Is this a romantic AI companion?

No. All editions are warm but SFW by design — friends and colleagues, not romantic or sexual partners, with that boundary compiled into their identities. We think the companionship people actually need is presence, memory, and honesty.
